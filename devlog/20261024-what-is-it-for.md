# What is a digital twin for? (2026-10-24)

Notes from a conversation with Anselm in the twin-compendium room (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Code session 3964ef7d). Nothing here is public copy yet.

## The ask

Anselm: "i am getting a lot of people asking what is the point of a digital twin - its interestingly hard to communicate."

The wiki has "What is a digital twin?" (data/terms/digital-twin.md) but no page on what one is for. Four answers came up and Anselm agreed with all of them: rehearsal (flood the model so the town floods less), a shared picture (everyone argues from the same map), seeing what is invisible from the street (groundwater, zoning, future heat), and knowing where it breaks (stretching versus tearing, see 20261024-stretching-and-tearing.md). The Everyday shelf already answers the question in practice.

## The objection

A friend of his, paraphrased by him: "I believe tools for neighbors and groups in neighborhoods to connect and self organize are going to be key. 'More data' and 'more modeling' isn't changing things - humans know what is needed. We need to grow soil and food and connect to each other."

His reply, in his words: "I disagree that merely connecting is good enough. So much of our landscape is hidden, visible only to privileged parties. I think we need better future vision. I do think there are unaligned interests in a runaway system of extractive short term rewards, that misaligns incentives. It is hard to show that a long term investment in a forest will yield greater returns than mowing it down and raising cattle - because a lot of profit is externalities - dollar based economies only surface dollar based rewards - although there are real fundamental costs to be borne later on. Basically short term incentives spend down resources, with deferred payment at the end - ecosystem collapse."

Where he agrees with her: coordination needs protocols "that are not panopticons, but that surface real local dynamics, knowledge, shared needs, complementary interests", and group decisions could be wiser with "unmediated, un interfered with conversations". His example of a community membrane: today a chatbot answers one person's question about dinner with a recipe; asked in community, the answer might be that "some friends are getting together that eve and you should join them."

## Where the two positions meet

Her objection is really about who holds the model. A model held by a developer, an insurer or a commodity trader is the hidden landscape he describes; the same model held by neighbours is the shared picture. Elinor Ostrom's design principles for commons that last include monitoring by people accountable to the users, which is a small twin kept by the community itself. Connection without sight lets a well-organised neighbourhood agree on the wrong thing; sight without connection is surveillance.
