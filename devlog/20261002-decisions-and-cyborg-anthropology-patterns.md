## Cyborg Anthropology wiki, read today

https://cyborganthropology.com/index.php/Main_Page (main page only; fetched
2026-10-02). What the page itself states: established 2010; 1,261 articles;
MediaWiki; last edited 25 August 2026; CyborgCamp conference hosted since
2008; a companion book, "An Illustrated Dictionary of Cyborg Anthropology",
described as "a more portable version of some of this wiki's highlighted
concepts"; a TED talk; a "Suggest site content" link; a donations prompt.

Patterns worth taking:

1. **The site defines the field by naming it.** The first section is "What
   is Cyborg Anthropology?". The wiki is where the term lives.
2. **Entries are named concepts.** Short, memorable handles: Panic
   Architecture, Email Apnea, Digital Hoarding, Ambient Awareness. The
   vocabulary is the product. Tools age; a good name for a recurring
   situation lasts and gets cited.
3. **One caretaker's taste over many years.** Sixteen years, still edited.
   It reads as curated, not crowd-sourced, and outside pieces are credited
   by name ("Submitted by ...").
4. **Unfinished work is shown.** "In Development" and "New Territory" lists
   sit on the main page. Same idea as growing a seed in public.
5. **A teaching section.** Course materials, sample assignments, a reading
   list offered as the place to start.
6. **A constellation around the wiki.** Talk, book, conference. The wiki is
   the hub; the other forms carry it to people who will never browse a wiki.

Patterns not to take:

- The main page is a wall of link lists with no path through it. A newcomer
  is not told where to begin beyond the reading list.
- It is organised by the maintainer's interests, not the reader's situation.
  That suits curious readers of ideas. Our reader arrives with a problem.
- No plain layer and technical layer; no machine-readable form.

## What this changes for us

- **Concepts become first-class, as named patterns.** One page per recurring
  idea or situation in modeling a place, each with a memorable plain name, a
  one-line definition, an example, and links to instruments and cases. This
  is the authored layer and the part most likely to be quoted. "Stewardship
  twin" and "sensory landscape" from the survey are the first two. Water has
  its own existing vernacular worth documenting, for example "paper water"
  against "wet water" (rights on paper against water actually in the river;
  I believe this is established usage in western US water law, from memory,
  to be sourced before it is written).
- **An "in development" list on the home page**, generated from `proposed`
  and `hidden` entries, as the public invitation.
- **A teaching page per topic** once there is material: how to run a session
  with a group using one of the free tools.
- **A portable edition later.** The yearly state-of-the-field idea is the
  analogue of her book.

The home page still opens with the reader's question, not with the
definition of the field. "What is a digital twin?" is the first concept
page, one tap away.
