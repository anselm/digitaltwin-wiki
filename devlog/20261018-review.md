## Against each principle

**Actionable, not passive: fails.** The pages built to start from a need,
the guides and the questions, have no text. A question page such as
Flooding does link three engines, but two of the three are vendors, none
is flagged as open or runnable, and no data source or twin is linked. A
guest with a problem gets a list of names and no next step. The one thing
on the site a guest can act on is Bay Layers, because it runs and its
source is on GitHub.

**Evergreen: passes in shape.** No section is dated news; Recent is
separate; Vitals refreshes itself. What is written (the books, a dozen
engine entries with real bodies, Bay Layers, Valdrada, the climate report
article) will read the same in a year.

**Culled: fails.** Engines is one alphabetical list of 79, 34 with a body
under 40 words. 36 entries still carry the survey's lineage sentence
("Listed in the survey under ... Audiences named there: ..."), which the
writing rules forbid. The LinkedIn-imported entries are clean of lineage
text; the survey ones were never rewritten.

**The bar (factual, sourced, neutral, unauthored): passes where there is
text.** Every engine, book, data source and term lists a source. No
bylines in bodies. The survey lineage text is the one systematic breach.
Guides and questions have no sources because they have no claims.

**Not fixated on "digital twin": fails in the chrome, passes in the
entries.** The site is called Digital Twins Wiki, the twins section is
labelled "Digital twins", the Guides section is described as "So you want
to make a digital twin?", and the home text uses the phrase five times.
Only nine entry labels or descriptions use it, four of them book titles
that do. The name is Anselm's and the chrome is his call.

**Start by need: fails by configuration.** The Questions section, the one
section organised by what a guest wants to know, is out of the nav
(`nav: false`), and so is About. A guest sees kinds (Engines, Books,
Data) and never sees the questions. The home page has pins and domains
and no doors.

**Machines propose, people promote: works.** Everything an agent wrote is
draft; staging says so in the corner and is noindex; production would
show about thirty entries.

**Who this is for, stated publicly: already exists.** `about/audience`,
written 2026-10-02 from Anselm's intentions and still proposed, names the
guests in plain words (someone who farms, someone at a town or agency, a
group of neighbours, people who build these models) and the aims. It
matches the patrons entry closely, including the "nowhere to go" test. It
promises "technical terms explained where they appear", which Anselm has
since said is too high a bar. It is reachable only through /about, which
is not in the nav.

## Done the same day, on Anselm's "do as you suggest"

Anselm, 2026-10-18: do all six; write a README about the idea; commit,
push, go live; revise if there are issues. On drafts: fine, we are
sketching. So everything below is draft and shown on staging.

1. **Questions and About are in the nav.** Questions first, since a guest
   starts from a need; About last.
2. **The ten question pages and nine guides are written**, for the person
   with a mandate, from entries that exist on this site and nothing else.
   Each question page splits the question, names the kind of model, the
   data, what to do without a model, and the next step. The guides read in
   stage order and each ends with the next. Where an open tool the page
   needs is not yet on the site (microclimate, fire spread, noise), the
   page says so rather than naming one unsourced. Anselm has not read them.
3. **The 36 survey-origin entries were rewritten from the makers' pages**
   by three agents under a written brief (`scratchpad/rewrite-brief.md`,
   not kept): lineage sentence gone, body from pages read, sources
   retitled to the pages' own titles, `audiences` dropped. Their reports
   are summarised below.
4. **Engines are shelved by task.** Fourteen groups, set on every engine
   in `tool.group`; the list view groups when most items carry one, with a
   jump bar. Guides sort by stage.
5. **Pins** are five launching-off points: Who this is for, Name the
   question, Flooding, Build the smallest version, Bay Layers. Anselm's
   four empty articles are unpinned and untouched; they carry his byline
   and are his to write.
6. **The chrome is unchanged**, except the Guides blurb no longer leads
   with the phrase. The site name and the twins label are his.

`README.md` now states the idea, the patrons, the arrangement, how it is
made, the rules for agents, and the devlog method.
