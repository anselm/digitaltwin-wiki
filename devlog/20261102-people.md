# People: seminal figures get entries

2 November 2026. Anselm, after Mike Liebhold's note on Karen Willcox: "We can add seminal figures to the wiki - makes sense - we ourselves are also people in the space. Do an audit for names and people and add a file path for them and link to entries. Don't bother with a catalog of people yet directly from site - ie we don't have to index."

Done by Claude (Opus 5.5) in session https://claude.ai/code/session_01QvbEMPfgTgBCEh7BrFsFv1, with one audit agent and five writing agents.

## The audit

An agent read the store (554 entries, skipping people/ and about/) and found 676 distinct people. Almost all are named once: 615 in one entry, 43 in two, 12 in three, 5 in four, Michael Batty in six. So the choice rests on who is seminal to modelling places, systems and futures, not on counts. The full table (name, slug, entries, role) was kept in the session scratchpad, not the repo; rerunning the audit is cheap.

Links: the first mention in each entry's prose now links the person's page (88 links), and works they wrote, edited or built carry a made_by relation to them (54), so each person page lists their works through the reverse "makes". The People shelf stays out of the navigation, as asked.

## Left out where sources disagreed

- Forrester's first system dynamics study: Sprague Electric (Wikipedia) or a GE appliance plant (MIT obituary).
- The Limits to Growth: 1971 (donellameadows.org eulogy) or 1972 (Wikipedia); the entry uses 1972. Founding year of the Sustainability Institute, 1996 or 1997.
- Mirror Worlds: 1991 (publisher) or 1992/1993 (Wikipedia); no year given.
- Grieves dates the digital twin idea to 2003 in his own paper; speaker bios say 2002. The entry gives 2003 as his claim.
- Toffler's Future Shock sales (6 million or 15 million); Fuller's book count (28 or 30+); Rosen's sabbatical year; Webber's 1964 or 1965 paper and his school town; Schelling's PhD year; Cowan's Los Alamos start year.
- Who founded the Institute for Alternative Futures: its own site names Toffler, Dator and Bezold; Wikipedia, Bezold alone.

## Doubts worth a human look

- Gelernter: Wikipedia reports Yale removed his teaching duties in 2026; the entry lists Yale as affiliation and leaves the matter out.
- Jessica Talisman's portrait is a CC0 "own work" upload by a user whose identity is not confirmed.
- Lansing's portrait is an "own work" upload credited to Steve Lansing.
- Thin sources: Shafto (NASA reports only), Glaessgen (a 2015 seminar notice), Etienne (a 2014 note), Bousquet's current unit.
- Founders of the Santa Fe Institute (Gell-Mann, Cowan, Pines) are member_of SFI, not its makers.
- Anselm, Howard and Mike already had entries as contributors; none of the new entries mention them.
