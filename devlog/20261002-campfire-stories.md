# 2026-10-02 — campfire stories

Point-in-time entry, tenth of the day. Written by Claude.

## Anselm's term

He opens with onboarding he calls "campfire stories": stories "we all know
already but we love them". His survey starts this way: transport planners
testing a road closure, utilities looking at electric vehicles and heat
pumps, fire agencies estimating where a wildfire will spread, communities
exploring proposals for where they live. (Survey rechecked today; unchanged
since this morning's copy in `seed/`.)

I had the home page opening on a question box. That skipped the campfire.
A newcomer needs to recognise what this is before being asked what they want.

## What it becomes in the design

- **A new kind: `corpus/stories/`.** A story is short, true, sourced, and
  told plainly: a place, something happening, what the model let someone
  see. It links to the instruments, concepts and cases behind it.
- **Home page order is now: title, three stories, then the question box.**
  Story first, then "what about your place?".
- **Every topic opens with its own story.** Water needs one or two. His
  river work is the obvious source; I have no facts for it and have written
  nothing.
- **Stories are also the conversational opener.** An assistant asked "what
  is this for?" tells one, and cites it.

Stories already present in the survey, each with a source there:

- Tees Valley's traffic twin and the late bus (sources 2, 3).
- The flooded substation that takes water pumping and communications with
  it (CReDO, sources 38, 39).
- The cycle lane walk-through (Appendix B; illustrative, not a real case).
- The farming watershed as a changing mosaic (Appendix F; illustrative).
- Pedregal frogs and aircraft noise (Serrano and Ochoa-Ochoa), with Bernie
  Krause's account as the vivid version the survey is careful to qualify.
- Limits to Growth, 1972, as the old story of the field (source 72).
