# An article on how people respond to what a model shows them

16 November 2026. Claude (Fable 5.2), in the twin-compendium room, session 018TGCFhFpKfqJ5fkxug9aM4.

Anselm sent Suldovsky, Vaala and Lapierre (Climate 14:211, 2026) on eco-paralysis among young Americans and asked whether the wiki should track human factors, "related to communication and usability", though it is outside the engines. Claude recommended a narrow form and he answered "Let's at least have an article yes."

Written: `data/articles/how-people-respond-to-what-a-model-shows.md`, a draft. It is linked from the guide "Put it in front of its users" and from the Communication shelf's index page.

What it rests on: ten sources. Read in full: Suldovsky 2026 (from the PDF he sent), Innocenti 2023 in IJERPH, Vlasceanu 2024 and Rooney-Varga 2018 (the last three as full text from Europe PMC). Abstract only: Innocenti 2023 in Climate, Tannenbaum 2015, O'Neill and Nicholson-Cole 2009, Retchless 2018, Rooney-Varga 2020. Mildenberger 2024 in Nature Sustainability was read only through the authors' summary on the Yale Program on Climate Change Communication site, because Nature sits behind a login. Each source line says which.

Decisions and limits:

- The section "What a builder can take from this" is the article's own reading and says so. No study was found that tested a dashboard or a model of a particular place on its audience.
- van Dijk and colleagues 2026 on an inverted-U relation between anxiety and action is cited by Suldovsky but has no open abstract, so it is not used.
- The survey scales are not entered as separate entries. The article points to the appendix where they are printed. An instrument entry is still open if he wants it.
- Not started: any shelf or tag for human factors. The article carries the tag `human factors` and sits in the People and Climate domains.
