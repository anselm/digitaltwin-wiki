## The distinction, as a rule for every page

Her wiki describes; a reader leaves knowing more. This guide directs; a
reader leaves with a next step. So every page ends in something to do:

- an instrument page ends in "try it" or "who to ask";
- a question page ends in the shortest route to an answer;
- a playbook stage ends in a checklist and a template to fill in;
- a flow ends in a destination, never in "it depends".

Concept pages (the pattern borrowed from her wiki) stay, as the layer that
explains. They are not the way in.

## Flow diagrams as a first-class kind

- `corpus/flows/<id>.md`: a flow is data, not a drawing. Nodes are questions
  with yes and no edges; leaves are links to entries. One renderer draws
  every flow in the same style, and the same file is the script an assistant
  follows when it asks the questions in conversation.
- Style, following the survey's diagram habits: questions in outlined boxes
  down a spine, "no" continues down, "yes" exits right to a filled box that
  is a link. Dark labels, one accent, no quantities implied. Reads top to
  bottom on a phone without scrolling sideways.
- First flow, in the mockup now: "So you want to make a digital twin?" Five
  questions (not sure what to ask; already modeled; map or spreadsheet
  enough; free tool exists; data and a little code), six exits (a story,
  look at theirs, do that instead, use it today, adapt an open model, build
  one). The questions and their order are my proposal.
- Flows to draw next, one per playbook stage where there is a real choice:
  which kind of model; where to find data for a place; how to check a
  result; what to do about data that identifies people.
