## Neighbours (checked by web search today, summaries only, sites not read)

There are institutional voices. Each is tied to a membership, a country or a
programme.

- **Digital Twin Consortium** (digitaltwinconsortium.org). A membership body,
  described in an April 2026 presentation as an EDM Association community.
  Working groups, a glossary, a "capabilities periodic table", an open-source
  listing, testbeds. Working-group names in the search results lean to
  engineering and product development.
- **Digital Twin Hub** (digitaltwinhub.co.uk). UK. Moved from the Centre for
  Digital Built Britain to Connected Places Catapult on 1 April 2022. Carries
  the Gemini Principles (2018): nine commitments under Purpose, Trust and
  Function, for a national infrastructure twin.
- **EU Local Digital Twin Toolbox** (interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu,
  collection ldttoolbox). For EU public administrations. Eleven tools, a
  solutions catalogue, a knowledge hub, a marketplace, with the Open & Agile
  Smart Cities network.

What I did not find in three searches: an independent, practitioner-level
reference that is neutral across vendors and programmes, covers ecological
and watershed work alongside urban and infrastructure, is free to read in
full, and is built for LLMs to use. Three searches is thin evidence of
absence. These neighbours should be read properly and become entries, and
the guide should link to them generously.

## Parallels and what each would lend (from my own knowledge, not checked today)

- **MDN** for web developers: reference plus guides, neutral across browser
  vendors, with the vendors themselves contributing. Closest model.
- **CNCF Landscape**: one map of every tool in a field, by category.
- **Thoughtworks Technology Radar**: a periodic, opinionated reading of what
  to adopt, trial, assess or hold. A model for a yearly edition.
- **Teacher-resource sites**: practitioners sharing worked material that
  others can reuse as is. The analogue here is reusable artefacts: interview
  guides, data-inventory templates, verification checklists, scoping briefs.

## What the civic framing changes

1. **Audience.** Builders first (the "us" in his message), then groups
   considering a twin. The earlier drafts addressed buyers.
2. **A practitioner directory** becomes a natural section: who builds these,
   for what domains, with which tools. He is listed like anyone else.
3. **Reusable artefacts** join catalogue, playbook and cases as a fourth kind
   of content.
4. **Voice.** A reference describes; a voice takes positions (what counts as
   verified, what privacy practice is acceptable). One person cannot speak
   for a field on day one. The reference comes first, and positions come
   later out of the contributors it gathers. Public copy should say "kept by
   practitioners", never "the voice of".
5. **Home and licence.** A commons needs an open licence and a neutral home.
   Whether that is orbital.foundation, a new name, or his own is his call; I
   do not know orbital.foundation's legal status.

## Draft front-page line under this framing

> Digital Twins of Places
> A shared reference for people who build models of cities, watersheds and
> regions: the tools, the methods, the worked examples, and what has been
> learned. Free to read, free to reuse, open to correction.
