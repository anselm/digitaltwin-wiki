# Coverage against other people's lists (2026-10-21)

Anselm asked how TESSERA, Cambridge's open satellite-embedding model, was missed, and whether outside collections could make the wiki more complete. It was missed because every entry so far came from one of our own channels: his survey, the LinkedIn scrape, the inbox, Mike Liebhold's syntheses. Nothing compared the wiki against a list made by someone else.

## What was tried

- Wikipedia: no TESSERA article, no Category:Digital_twins, and its software lists are general GIS packages. Weak for this.
- Curated GitHub lists: Jack-bo1220/Awesome-Remote-Sensing-Foundation-Models and satellite-image-deep-learning/techniques both list TESSERA. sacridini/Awesome-Geospatial and acgeospatial/awesome-earthobservation-code list neither TESSERA nor AlphaEarth.

## The tool

`tools/coverage.js <file or GitHub URL>` reads a markdown list, takes the first link on each line, and checks it against data/ by URL (GitHub by owner/repo, arXiv by paper id, Hugging Face by owner/model), then by name: the whole name or its first word, on shelves that hold things, never on topic shelves such as domains or questions. A looser "the line mentions one of our names" rule was tried and dropped: it matched OSMGen to OpenStreetMap. Gaps print under the list's own headings. Zero dependencies.

## First run

- Awesome-Remote-Sensing-Foundation-Models: 234 links, 4 here, 230 not.
- satellite-image-deep-learning/techniques: 1,729 links, 8 here, 1,721 not.

Both are research catalogues, mostly papers. The wiki does not try to cover every mention, so a raw gap list is not a worklist. Two readers shortlisted the items a practitioner could use today (released, licensed, durable maker), each checked against its own page; the shortlists go to Anselm, and entries are written from the projects' pages as always. Gap lists are in inbox/coverage/.

Lists better matched to this wiki would be about modelling places rather than machine learning on imagery: urban simulation, hydrology, open city data. Finding them is the next use of the tool.
