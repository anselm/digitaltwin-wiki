# Who pays for a civic twin (2026-10-23)

Anselm shared a summary of YC's Summer 2026 requests for startups (a
pasted digest, not YC's page; not checked against it) and asked what is
pertinent. Then: "note also their focus is on sellable products. If it
cannot be trivially productized with extractive revenue - they won't fund
it."

## What lines up

- "Run the smallest towns end-to-end." The wiki already has the case:
  `scenarios/hartman-colorado-water-system`, a town of under 60 people
  dissolved while its 40-home water system failed.
- "Software for agents." The wiki is machine-readable already, and Mireye
  (`data-sources/mireye`, YC S26) sells US place facts to agents with a
  source on every value. A supplier or a competitor, not open ground.
- The digest's through-line, that durable businesses sell "verification,
  accountability, licensure", is the wiki's own discipline (every claim
  sourced, refuted, and a twin judged against what happened).

## Where it does not

YC's test is a product with revenue drawn from the people it serves.
Most of what makes a civic twin worth building fails that test on
purpose: the people with the most at stake (Hartman's residents, Alwar's
villages, Tuvalu) are the ones with least to pay, and charging them is
the opposite of the point. A venture-shaped version would sell to the
party with money in the decision (a utility, an insurer, a developer, a
lender), which is often the other side of the table from the residents.

## Hartman as a product sketch, either way

The scenario's third what-if is the sellable one: which other small water
systems show the same pattern (years of deficiencies, penalties, a vacant
certified operator, unspent grants) before they collapse. The buyers would
be the bodies that carry the risk, not the town: the state regulator and
the state's local-affairs grant office, the lender that financed the
system (USDA in Hartman's case, per the scenario's sources), the county
that inherits it. That is public money buying an early-warning service,
not extractive revenue, and it is the honest shape for most of the
scenarios on the shelf.

## Funding that fits civic work (from general knowledge, not checked)

Government contracts and grants (the agencies above; science funders);
philanthropy that funds civic technology and journalism; open source with
paid services and hosting for the agencies that adopt it; and a split
where an open public layer is free and a professional layer (the
lender's or insurer's view) is paid. Each needs its own reading before
it goes anywhere public.

## For the engine

Pick one buyer and one decision first, as the digest says, even if the
buyer is a public body: the early-warning list for small water systems
is a candidate first runnable benchmark case, with the state's own
violation records to check it against.
