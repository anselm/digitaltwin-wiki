- Google, "We're supporting nature restoration through sustainable farming
  in Brazil", 2026-10-19,
  https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/brazil-silvopasture/
  Google, through the Symbiosis Coalition, contracts 270,000 tonnes of
  carbon removal from Working Trees, which plants trees on active pasture
  in the Amazon and Cerrado; every tree is geotagged. The coalition's own
  note (https://www.symbiosiscoalition.org/perspectives/offtakes-working-trees,
  2026-10-19, Julia Strong) says Google and McKinsey together contracted
  320,000 tonnes over ten years, restoring 8,700 hectares inside a 50,000
  hectare project, and quotes a global estimate of 0.3 to 1 billion tonnes
  of CO2 a year from silvopasture. Working Trees' site
  (https://workingtrees.com/) describes remote-sensing project design and
  satellite and machine-learning MRV, aims of a million hectares by 2035.
  Reading: a corporate announcement, not a resource. The question under it
  is a model question: what trees on a given ranch do to carbon, shade,
  soil and drought over decades, and whether a geotag per tree is enough to
  verify it. Belongs as a cited example on the land-use-change use case or
  in the things-to-model list, not as an entry, unless Working Trees
  publishes its methods or data.

## US deep-sea mining claims in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone (Mongabay, 2026-10-20)

https://news.mongabay.com/custom-story/2026/10/us-deep-sea-mining-claims-in-pacifics-high-seas-now-rival-the-area-set-aside-by-international-regulator/
Elizabeth Claire Alberts and Andrés Alegría, 20 October 2026. Sent by Anselm.

What it says: since the April 2025 executive order, at least seven companies
filed about a dozen applications with NOAA under the 1980 Deep Seabed Hard
Mineral Resources Act; nine look active, six are published in the Federal
Register. Mongabay mapped them: about 727,738 km² of the Clarion-Clipperton
Zone sought, 962,634 km² with Lockheed Martin's two 1984 licences, about half
the CCZ area the ISA oversees for mining and nearly 80% of its 17 exploration
contracts there (1.23 million km²). Claims overlap ISA contracts, ISA reserved
areas and each other. One application (Deep Sea Minerals Corp., 147,430 km²)
has no published geometry and is left out. The story has an interactive map.

Reading: fuel, not an entry. Two model questions sit under it, and they are
different kinds:
- Who claims which seabed, and where do the claims collide. A GIS overlay of
  polygons from the Federal Register and the ISA. Mongabay has done it once.
- What mining a nodule field does to the abyss and the water above it:
  sediment plumes, how far they travel, how long the floor takes to recover.
  That is an ocean model question and the one that fits the wiki.
Pages to read before any entry: the ISA's own data (DeepData) and contract
maps, the NOAA applications in the Federal Register, and the DeepCCZ
expedition (NOAA) named in a photo credit. Not read yet.
