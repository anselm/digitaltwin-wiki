Model Your Place

Tools for seeing how a farm, a town, a watershed or a region behaves, and what would change if you did something differently. What you can use today, what it costs, and how others have done it.

  • A late bus

    In Tees Valley, the traffic centre runs a live model of the roads. It spots a bus falling behind and helps staff change the signals ahead of it.

    Tees Valley Combined Authority, August 2025
  • A flooded substation

    Water reaches one electrical substation. Water pumping and communications can go down with it. A connected model shows the damage spreading before it happens.

    CReDO, Connected Places Catapult
  • A frog chorus

    Aircraft noise changes how Pedregal frogs call and thins out their chorus. A flight path is part of a pond's surroundings too.

    Serrano and Ochoa-Ochoa, cited in the survey

What do you want to see?

  • MovementWhere will vehicles go after a closure, and can signal changes help a late bus? Aimsun · SUMO · MATSim · Tees Valley's traffic twin
  • PeopleHow will people move through a station or leave an area during an emergency? AnyLogic · GAMA
  • EnergyWhere will electrification require more network capacity, storage or generation? Neara · PyPSA · Isle of Wight energy demonstrator
  • WaterWhich areas will flood, and how will changes to rivers, drainage or pumps affect them? Deltares Delft3D · DHI · Xylem Vue
  • FireWhere might a fire spread, which assets are exposed, and where would mitigation help? Technosylva
  • PlacesHow might zoning or transport investment change development and household location? UrbanSim · Urbanly CityCompass
  • Food and landHow might land-use changes affect water, habitat, carbon or agricultural production? InVEST · DSSAT · GCAM · Earth Index
  • WeatherWhat conditions are likely next week, and how might a region's climate change over decades? WeatherNext · NVIDIA Earth-2 · Destination Earth
  • SensesHow do sound, light, smell and barriers affect people and other animals? Walk Score · CadnaA · Radiance · GRAL · Circuitscape
  • Deciding togetherWhat changes when we combine different climate policies or land-use proposals? En-ROADS · Geodesignhub

Design noteThese ten questions and the tools beside them are taken as written from the survey's applications table. For this audience they need rewriting in plainer words. Each one opens a page listing the instruments, data and examples behind that answer.

You may not need to build anything

  1. Look at a model that already exists for your place

    Helsinki publishes its city model and an energy atlas in the browser.

  2. Use a free tool

    En-ROADS lets anyone combine climate policies and see the modeled effect.

  3. Adapt an open model

    SUMO, MATSim, PyPSA and InVEST are open and can be set up for a new place.

  4. Commission a build

    Tees Valley worked with Aimsun on a traffic twin now used in its traffic management centre.

If you do build one

Nine stages, each with a checklist, what you can do yourselves, and when to bring in help.

  1. Name the question
  2. Talk to the people affected
  3. Find the data
  4. Choose the kind of model
  5. Build the smallest version
  6. Check it against what happened
  7. Protect people's privacy
  8. Put it in front of its users
  9. Keep it alive

Design noteThe stages are a proposal and have not been checked against how real projects ran. Each opens a page with a small flow diagram and a checklist. The same checklists are packaged for an AI assistant to walk a group through.
