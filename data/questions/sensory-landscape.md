Someone wants to know what a new road, runway, lit car park or fence would do to a place, or why a place already feels wrong. The change reaches everyone who can hear, see or smell it, and the animals that navigate by those senses.

## What you are really asking

- **How far does the noise, the light or the smell reach**, and at what level?
- **Who notices**: people trying to sleep, birds timing their song, frogs calling, insects navigating by the sky?
- **What would a change do**: a barrier, dimmer lights, rerouted traffic or quiet hours?

## What kind of model answers it

Noise and light propagate in ways that are well understood. **Propagation models** map them from their sources across terrain and buildings. The harder half is the effect: what a given level does to a species or a neighbourhood. Field studies answer that, not physics. Agent-based models simulate many individual animals moving by simple rules, and are used to ask what a barrier or a corridor does to where animals can go.

On this site: [CadnaA](/engines/cadnaa/) for noise, [Radiance](/engines/radiance/) for light, [GRAL](/engines/gral/) for odour and pollutant dispersion, [Circuitscape](/engines/circuitscape/) for habitat connectivity and barriers, and [Walk Score](/engines/walk-score/) for how a place reads on foot. Each entry says what is open and what is not. [A frog chorus](/stories/a-frog-chorus/) is a short story of what one such study found. [Blue Corridors](/data-sources/blue-corridors-bluecorridors-org/) is the ocean version: a map of whale migration routes with the threats laid alongside.

## What you need

- The sources: where the road, the runway and the lights are, and their schedule.
- Terrain and buildings.
- Who and what is there: residents, and species records such as [iNaturalist](/data-sources/inaturalist/) observations.
- Measurements: a sound meter on a few nights is cheap and is the check on any model.

## Without a model

Go at night. Listen and look. Ask residents when it is worst. A map of what you heard, hour by hour, is what a model would produce.
