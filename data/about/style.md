The wiki is a reference written for people who build models of places, and for people who are deciding whether to. Software agents draft most pages from sources and a person checks them before they are promoted. Until then a page is marked as a draft.

The reader the pages have in mind is intelligent and interested and is often not a specialist. A technical term is explained where it first appears, and a page tries to say why a thing matters and to whom, as well as what it is.

Two kinds of page use that voice differently.

**Entries** describe a tool, a dataset, a model, a paper or a person: what it is, what it does, who uses it, what it needs and where it falls short. An entry takes no side and favours no vendor. It names the pages it was written from and the date they were last read. A statement is marked as what it is, whether a measurement, a model result, a maker's claim or a guess, and what is not known is said to be unknown.

**Position pages** are the few on the About shelf where the site holds a view: that models of places which run forward in time are worth building in the open, and that people outside the professions are among their users. The [objections](/about/objections/) page is the main one. A position page states the view as the site's own, gives the evidence, puts the opposing case in terms its holders would recognise, and concedes what is true in it.

An earlier version of this page, published on 11 November 2026, set out twenty-one rules of prose. They were withdrawn the next day, because pages written to them came out clipped and harder to follow than the pages they replaced. [What has changed](/about/changes/) records both steps.

A reader who finds a page that lectures, sells, pads or is hard to follow can report it through [Contributors](/about/contributors/).
