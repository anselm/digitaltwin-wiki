A short record of how the site has changed, newest first. The full reasoning behind each step is in the repository's dated devlog.

## 2026

**19 October.** Technologies is arranged as a stack, in order: Data, Ontologies, Engines, Presentation. Ontologies and Presentation are new shelves; a term entry on ontology and an article, The parts of a digital twin, say why. A review of Jessica Talisman's book Ontology Pipeline joins the Ontologies shelf, with her browser SKOS editor beside it.

**18 October.** Books become Media, with the podcasts and video channels beside them. A Mandates section begins, with the 30 by 30 target as its first entry. Every engine, twin and data source gains an open block on source, data, API and participation. Eleven notable twins written from their own pages, among them eReefs, the European Digital Twin of the Ocean, Moorea IDEA, the NSW Spatial Digital Twin, Rotterdam, Zürich and Boston. The sidebar is arranged in areas: new, practice, technologies, theory, resources. Questions become use cases and sit after the guides; engines are shelved by the task a guest is trying to do. The ten use-case pages and nine guides are written. Thirty-six entries carried over from the survey are rewritten from their makers' pages. The survey's diagrams and credited images are placed on entries. A written statement of who the site is for and the bar an entry must clear. Sources widen beyond the phrase "digital twin" to a shared vocabulary of models and simulations of places; video and podcast sources join the scanner. Six planetary vital signs, refreshed at every deploy. The repository is renamed digitaltwin-wiki.

**17 October.** The repository goes public. digitaltwins.wiki points at the site. Bay Layers, a layered scene of San Francisco Bay, becomes the home page figure. Eighty-one entries are imported as drafts from a catalogue mined from LinkedIn posts, and a scanner begins polling papers, code, news and communities into an inbox for editors.

**15 October.** The site is rebuilt from scratch on a small, generic knowledge store: every entry a markdown file whose frontmatter keys are its components, with a validator, a query API and machine exports. The first build is archived.

**2 October.** The project begins. A September survey of the field, [Digital Twins: A Survey](https://digital-twins-survey.anselm52491.chatgpt.site/), is split into its first entries; the ten domains and their visual vocabulary are adopted from it; the audience is stated as ordinary people with a real question about a place; a first static site goes to staging.
