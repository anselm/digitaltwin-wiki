Its Spanish name is Laboratorio Agroecológico Abierto. Agroecology is farming that works with the local ecosystem. The lab is a loose grouping of rural groups, technical teams, researchers and activists. Alongside its tools it runs participatory processes for farms making that transition. Partners include Cooperativa Ayllu, reGOSH, INTA and two faculties of the Universidad Nacional de Cuyo. reGOSH is a Latin American open-hardware network. INTA is Argentina's national agricultural technology institute. Three funders are named; one is the Shuttleworth Foundation. Fernando "Nano" Castro of Cooperativa Ayllu teaches at the lab.

The main product is an Open Source Soil Health Monitoring Kit. It combines open laboratory instruments and protocols with a companion app that walks a user through the tests and records results with standard metadata. Groups can then compare and interpret soil indicators together. Earlier work used OpenFlexure microscopes, an open-source 3D-printed design, with farmers in Mendoza to look at soil organisms. There were also soil chromatography workshops. The lab frames all this as collective learning among farmers and technicians as much as data collection.

The tools are open hardware and open software. In 2026 the kit, its protocols and its data recording were the subject of workshops with INTA Junín and the agronomy faculty.

Listed activities run from 2021 in Mendoza to DWeb Camp and the Kompost Festival in Germany in 2026. The kit was presented at the DWeb virtual meetup of September 2026.
