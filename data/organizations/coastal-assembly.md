The company grew out of ten years of wave-tank experiments and simulations at the lab. That work showed that the forces stripping sand from a beach can be guided to put it back. It was established in 2017. Skylar Tibbits is CEO, Sarah Dole COO and Walter Zesk CTO. The team mixes physicists, architects and computer scientists, and works with marine biologists and Maldivian partners.

The modelling software ingests years of satellite imagery, seabed bathymetry (maps of water depth) and records of waves, currents, tides and swell. From these it extracts how a coastline has moved, projects where it is heading and flags the stretches eroding fastest. Every site studied stays on the map as a growing record of coastline behaviour. For each site the company designs the spacing and angle of the reef structures, casts them nearby and lowers them onto the seabed from a barge. Nothing needs running or maintaining afterwards, and the structures are meant to become reef habitat. Each site is then tracked against the model's forecast.

At the JW Marriott Maldives, fifty-four structures in a hexagonal pattern about thirty metres long were laid. The company credits them with ninety feet of new beach in six months without pumped sand, and says a later satellite pass showed the beach still gaining. The other client named is Taj Exotica. The service is commercial and sold by assessment. More projects are planned for Boston.
