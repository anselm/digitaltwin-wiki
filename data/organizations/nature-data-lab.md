The Nature Data Lab works on what it calls the nature data gap: measuring the state of nature is harder than measuring climate, with no single unit like the tonne of CO2 and, by its count, over 600 possible metrics across five domains.[^1] It is a fiscally sponsored project of [One Earth](/organizations/one-earth/) Philanthropy, a US charity, and works as a virtual research organization of fellows, research associates and technologists. Its people page lists about thirty, with Karl Burkart as CEO.[^3]

Its centerpiece is the [Global Safety Net](/data-sources/global-safety-net-v3-gsn3/), made with One Earth, which maps the world's remaining natural land and inland waters and their conservation status.[^1] The work grew from convenings in 2017 at the National Geographic Society and UNEP-WCMC, supported by the Leonardo DiCaprio Foundation.[^2] Those led to "A Global Deal for Nature" in 2019, the first peer-reviewed case for protecting 30% of the planet by 2030, then "A Global Safety Net" in 2020 and "Conservation Imperatives" in 2024.[^2]

Current projects include a 1990 to 2030 map of human modification of land, a third update of the world's 825 ecoregions, a Conservation Status Index for Target 3 of the Global Biodiversity Framework, and an Iconic Species Index of key vertebrate populations.[^4] The lab counts 44 studies, white papers and open data products it has led, supported or co-authored.[^5] Fellows propose the research, and the lab raises money for it from philanthropists.[^1]
