Amazon.ia (Inteligencia Ambiental) covers the whole Amazon basin, about six million square kilometres. Its 28 partners are field stations, government institutes, companies, universities and NGOs. They include Planet Labs, the World Resources Institute and Smithsonian ForestGEO. Planet calls itself a founding partner. The Bezos Earth Fund is the founding funder, with more than US$14 million granted through the Andes Amazon Fund and WRI.

The program sets out to answer conservation questions with evidence:

- Is biodiversity in a place getting better or worse?
- Are protected areas working?
- Is an ecosystem recovering?
- Where is change happening?

The answers are to come as change alerts and indicators for conservationists, communities, researchers and policymakers.

Ground sensing is paired with Earth observation. Camera traps carry their own on-device compute, and the acoustic recorders run on solar power. DNA sequencing includes eDNA, the traces of DNA that animals shed into water and soil. Satellite imagery is the Earth-observation side. Cloud computing and AI classify species and fuse the different data streams. Planet is training AI on its own imagery and analytics for the project. The site refers to five monitoring sites at present.

Amazon.ia was announced on 23 September 2026 for launch in 2026. The partners say they are building the core data infrastructure and testing integrated models. No results are reported yet.
