In Climate X's Spectra, a user searches assets by postcode or uploads a portfolio, then picks a climate scenario.[^1] For each asset Spectra reports hazard probability, severity and estimated financial loss over short and long horizons.[^1] The output is formatted for disclosure frameworks such as IFRS S2 and the EU Taxonomy.[^1,2]

Spectra combines downscaled multi-model climate projections (global climate model output refined to a finer local grid) with Climate X's own building-vulnerability data and hazard models.[^1] Climate X says it covers over two billion assets worldwide.[^1] A global wildfire model at 30 m resolution is a recent addition.[^1] Customers are banks, real-estate firms and asset managers.[^1,2] Spectra is hosted software with an API.[^1] Climate X also sells Adapt, for costing adaptation measures, and Carta, for company-level exposure.[^2]

UrbanFootprint's Resilience Insights draw on a geospatial dataset for the whole United States that aligns climate hazard, community and household, and land-use and infrastructure data.[^3,4] UrbanFootprint cites 160 million parcels.[^4] Users evaluate hazard risk for one site or a portfolio, quantify community and financial impacts, and prioritise investment.[^3] Named users include an energy utility, Duquesne Light Company, and a community organisation, Fresno Metro Ministry, alongside financial institutions and government agencies.[^4] Delivery is as a cloud GIS (geographic information system: software for maps and spatial data) and dashboard application, and as data feeds.[^3,4]

Both are commercial and hosted.[^1,3] Neither publishes prices.
