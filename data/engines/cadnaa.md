The name stands for Computer Aided Noise Abatement. CadnaA models noise from industrial sites, road and railway networks, airports and whole towns. Its calculation standards include ISO 9613-2, the CNOSSOS-EU methods behind the EU Environmental Noise Directive, Germany's RLS-19 and TA Lärm, the UK's CRTN, the US TNM 2.5 and China's HJ2.4. Some are dated as recently as 2024. Sister products CadnaB and CadnaR cover building acoustics and room acoustics.

The typical uses all come before anything is built: strategic noise maps under the Environmental Noise Directive, noise studies for planning and permitting, industrial plant assessments, traffic noise forecasts, and the design of noise control measures such as barriers. Dedicated source types exist for wind turbines and for tennis, pickleball and padel courts. Models are built on the map or imported from CAD, GIS (shapefile, GeoPackage), OpenStreetMap, CityGML and raster terrain files. Results export back to GIS, Google Earth and office formats. Projects can run to 16 million objects per source type. DataKustik says consultancies, authorities and industry use it in more than 60 countries.

CadnaA is Windows-only software for x86-64 machines, not Apple silicon. It is sold in tiers from CadnaA Basic to CadnaA City & Airport, by one-time purchase or subscription, and licensed through a USB dongle or cloud licensing. Calculation can be spread over many cores and, with the CALC products, over other machines. A free demo version is offered.
