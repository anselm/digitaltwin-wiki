The programme's aim is to grow national capability in digital twinning and a working market for twins.[^1] Those twins should be safe, secure, trustworthy and ethical, able to exchange information with each other, and maintainable over time by organisations of any size.[^1] It is a government research and development programme,[^1] with nothing to install or buy.

The demonstrators on the Isle of Wight run in phases.[^1] The energy-system demonstrator models the island's electricity network from the 132 kV interconnectors down to 400 V transformers.[^2] A flexible marketplace for controllable heating, storage and e-mobility is added on top, to test how the island could become self-sufficient in renewable electricity and reach net zero by 2040.[^2] The stakeholders are Isle of Wight Council, Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks and the Isle of Wight Energy Hub.[^2]

The housing-retrofit demonstrator started in December 2022.[^3] It helps retrofit companies and third-sector organisations find homeowners eligible for funding such as ECO4, and put vulnerable households first.[^3] Its outputs are draft playbooks on user and data requirements, a carbon calculator for the data handling itself, and a visualisation tool.[^3] Pilots were planned from October 2023.[^3]

The asset-resilience demonstrator began in July 2022.[^4] The council, police, fire and rescue, the NHS trust and the ambulance service work with it on land slippage, flooding and other incidents.[^4] It has produced a tool for assessing infrastructure vulnerability and a visualisation tool for event planning and response.[^4]

The collection and case studies were published on 30 October 2023.[^1,2,3,4] Guidance on what a digital twin is and how organisations can contribute followed in January 2024.[^1]
