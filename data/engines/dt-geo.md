DT-GEO (Digital Twin for GEOphysical Extremes) is a European research project that builds simulation tools for geohazards: earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, tsunamis, and earthquakes caused by human activity such as geothermal energy projects and mining. It is coordinated by Geosciences Barcelona (GEO3BCN, part of the Spanish National Research Council CSIC), with Arnau Folch as project leader, and brings together 26 research institutions, among them the Barcelona Supercomputing Center, ETH Zürich, GFZ Potsdam, INGV, the Icelandic Meteorological Office and EPOS. Funding comes from the European Union's Horizon Europe programme under grant agreement 101058129.

The project is organised around twelve "digital twin components", each a bundle of simulation code, AI methods and live data feeds for one kind of question: four for volcanoes (unrest, ash clouds and fallout, lava flows, gases), one for probabilistic tsunami forecasting, six for earthquakes over long and short time scales, and one for forecasting human-induced seismicity. These are meant to serve early warning, hazard assessment and urgent computing on the EuroHPC supercomputing infrastructure, with outputs checked for FAIR data standards and compatible with the European Open Science Cloud (EOSC).

The components are tested at thirteen site demonstrators. Volcanoes: Etna in Italy, and Grímsvötn and Fagradalsfjall in Iceland. Tsunamis: the Mediterranean coast as a whole, eastern Sicily, the Chilean coast and the eastern Honshu coast of Japan, mostly by re-running past events such as the 2011 Tohoku tsunami. Earthquakes: the wider Euro-Mediterranean region, the central Apennines and the Alto-Tiberina fault in Italy, the Bedretto underground laboratory in Switzerland, and the Alps. Induced seismicity: a geothermal site in Strasbourg, France, and the KGHM copper mine in Poland.

The site describes the goal as a pre-operational prototype ready for future integration into Destination Earth, the European initiative to build a high-precision digital model of the Earth, and says observational data collected by DT-GEO partners will be fed into the DestinE twin to make it more accurate. The deliverables page lists first and second software releases and a sequence of reports, several marked as submitted.
