Climate Local is a set of web pages from Climate Central, one for each of a list of U.S. cities and states, that says how climate change shows up in that place. A reader searches for their city, or picks it or their state from the list. Climate Central does not give a total; counting the list for this page on 2026-10-23 found 247 cities, grouped under the 50 states and the District of Columbia, each of which has its own page. Climate Central says the coverage is large but limited, and suggests a nearby city or the state page when a city is missing.

The page for Birmingham, Alabama shows what a reader gets. At the top are three numbers, each with its data source named:

- average annual temperature up 3.9°F since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)
- annual precipitation up 3.6 inches since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)
- 58 days in 2026, as of 15 October, with temperatures made at least twice as likely by climate change ([Climate Shift Index](/communication/climate-shift-index/))

Then come the threats for the current season, which on that day was fall:

- 14 more days of extreme heat a year since 1970, counting days hotter than 95% of local temperatures in 1991 to 2020, which for Birmingham means above 93.15°F (NOAA ACIS)
- 26 billion-dollar hurricanes in Alabama from 1980 to 2025 (U.S. Billion Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters)
- a 37% rise from 1958 to 2021 in the rainfall of the heaviest 1% of rain events each year across the Southeast (Fifth National Climate Assessment)
- fall average temperatures up 3.7°F since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)

Below that, short cards connect the numbers to everyday matters (cooling schools, downpours, fall colour, warmer fall days, hurricanes, food prices), each linking to a fuller story in [Climate Matters](/communication/climate-matters/). At the foot of the page is a library of graphics localized for the city, for every season, which can be filtered by keyword and downloaded for use under Climate Central's terms. These allow any non-commercial use, commercial news use by media organizations and educational use by accredited educational bodies, with credit and, online, a link. Other commercial use, financial products in particular, needs written permission, and bulk downloading and scraping are forbidden.

Each page puts long-term records for one city next to a daily attribution count and a short list of what those changes mean there, in figures a reader can check against the named sources.
