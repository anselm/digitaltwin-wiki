Climate Local is a set of web pages from Climate Central, one for each of a list of U.S. cities and states, that says how climate change shows up in that place.[^1] A reader searches for their city, or picks it or their state from the list.[^1,3] Climate Central does not give a total; counting the list for this page on 2026-10-23 found 247 cities, grouped under the 50 states and the District of Columbia, each of which has its own page.[^3] Climate Central says the coverage is large but limited, and suggests a nearby city or the state page when a city is missing.[^1,3]

The page for Birmingham, Alabama shows what a reader gets. At the top are three numbers, each with its data source named[^2]:

- average annual temperature up 3.9°F since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)[^2]
- annual precipitation up 3.6 inches since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)[^2]
- 58 days in 2026, as of 15 October, with temperatures made at least twice as likely by climate change ([Climate Shift Index](/communication/climate-shift-index/))[^2]

Then come the threats for the current season, which on that day was fall[^2]:

- 14 more days of extreme heat a year since 1970, counting days hotter than 95% of local temperatures in 1991 to 2020, which for Birmingham means above 93.15°F (NOAA ACIS)[^2]
- 26 billion-dollar hurricanes in Alabama from 1980 to 2025 (U.S. Billion Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters)[^2]
- a 37% rise from 1958 to 2021 in the rainfall of the heaviest 1% of rain events each year across the Southeast (Fifth National Climate Assessment)[^2]
- fall average temperatures up 3.7°F since 1970 (NOAA ACIS)[^2]

Below that, short cards connect the numbers to everyday matters (cooling schools, downpours, fall colour, warmer fall days, hurricanes, food prices), each linking to a fuller story in [Climate Matters](/communication/climate-matters/).[^2] At the foot of the page is a library of graphics localized for the city, for every season, which can be filtered by keyword and downloaded for use under Climate Central's terms.[^2,4] These allow any non-commercial use, commercial news use by media organizations and educational use by accredited educational bodies, with credit and, online, a link.[^4] Other commercial use, financial products in particular, needs written permission, and bulk downloading and scraping are forbidden.[^4]

Each page puts long-term records for one city next to a daily attribution count and a short list of what those changes mean there, in figures a reader can check against the named sources.
