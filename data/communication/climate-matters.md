Climate Matters is a program of Climate Central, a hybrid science and media nonprofit in Princeton, New Jersey. The program sends TV meteorologists and journalists ready-made material for reporting on climate change where they live. Each week it produces data analysis, graphics localized for more than 245 U.S. cities and media markets, science explainers and other reporting resources on a timely topic, in English and Spanish. Media partners use them to tell stories about how climate change affects their own communities, and about ways to limit warming.

It began in 2010 as a pilot funded by the National Science Foundation, in partnership with George Mason University; during the pilot WXLT's Jim Gandy showed how climate change affects Columbia, South Carolina. Climate Central says the reason for working through weathercasters was that they are among the most trusted local science communicators. It became full time in 2012 with 12 meteorologists, by Climate Central's account; a 2020 paper listed on the same page dates the nationwide launch to 2013. Since then it has produced more than 560 weekly bulletins and more than 70,000 localized graphics, and Climate Central counts more than 3,000 meteorologists and journalists in its network. The same page also gives a figure of 200+ U.S. locations alongside the 245+ markets. The American Meteorological Society, NASA, NOAA and Climate Communication later joined as partners.

The program has been studied. A 2020 paper in the Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society reported a randomized controlled experiment in which localized climate reporting by TV weathercasters increased viewers' understanding of climate change as a local problem, across the political spectrum. Other papers listed on the page measured the program's effect on public understanding (Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society, 2020; Nature Climate Change, 2022) and tracked weathercasters' own views.

Climate Central's terms of use let anyone reuse its materials for any non-commercial purpose, commercial media organizations reuse them for a commercial news purpose, and accredited educational bodies for an educational purpose. Users must credit Climate Central and, online, link to the page the material came from. Other commercial use needs written permission, in particular any use to create, market or promote financial products or investments. Bulk downloading and screen scraping are forbidden. Images may be resized or trimmed in ways that do not affect the information, and text may be excerpted; other changes need written permission. The same organization publishes the [Climate Shift Index](/communication/climate-shift-index/) and city pages called [Climate Local](/communication/climate-local/), and its researchers wrote the [paper on extreme water levels](/articles/climate-central-extreme-water-levels/).

The program delivers a model's result through a person the audience already trusts, in the place the audience already looks.
