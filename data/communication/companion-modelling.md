Companion modelling, ComMod for short (in French, modélisation d'accompagnement), is a way of building a model of how people share a renewable resource, such as land, water or forest, together with the people concerned. The main tools are role-playing games and agent-based models, computer simulations in which each farmer, herder or household is a separate actor with its own rules. In practice a researcher draws up a first model of how the place works, puts it in front of the people concerned through simulations or role-playing games, and revises or rebuilds it from what they say and do. The cycle of field work, modelling, simulation and field work again repeats, and leaves a family of models rather than one finished one.

The approach was set out in 2003 as a charter in the Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation. Its twelve signatories came from CIRAD, Cemagref, INRA, the Tour du Valat and the IFB. Among them were the CIRAD modellers François Bousquet and Christophe Le Page, the Cemagref water scientist Olivier Barreteau and the INRA plant ecologist Michel Etienne. A French version was finalised in 2004, and a shorter text for a wider audience in 2013. The charter calls it a scientific stance rather than a modelling handbook, and the model "merely an intermediary object" for shared thinking.

The charter gives it two uses. One is to understand a system: researchers and local people learn together, and the model seeks "a mutual recognition of everyone representation" of the problem rather than simplifying what people know. The other is to support a collective decision, by making each party's points of view and criteria explicit. Here the charter says the aim is not the best solution but a better process, and that the scientific view is one among others. Every assumption behind the model is meant to be discarded after each round in the field, which is to say put up for refutation.

The group's main book in English is Companion Modelling: A Participatory Approach to Support Sustainable Development, edited by Michel Étienne (Springer, 2014, copyright Éditions Quae). A French collective book on the approach came out from Quae in 2010. The English book's chapters cover models for sharing representations, power imbalances between participants, assessing the effects, and passing the approach on. It includes 27 case studies and 7 teaching tools. The ComMod association runs a discussion forum and regular training schools; the 2026 school was held in Montpellier from 7 to 11 September with 13 participants. Cormas, CIRAD's agent-based modelling platform for relations between societies and their environment, is close to the group: the charter asked new signatories to register through the Cormas website, and the Cormas site offers the approach. Cormas is free under the MIT licence.

Here the people concerned meet the model while it is still being built, and what they say and do in the game changes it.
