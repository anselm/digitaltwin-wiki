World3 was built by a team at MIT. System dynamics models a system as quantities that build up or drain away, linked by feedback. In World3, population, industrial output, food, non-renewable resources and pollution act on one another through feedback loops. The team ran it under a range of assumptions.

The report presented the runs for a general reader. In most of them growth continues past the planet's limits and is followed by collapse within a century. A stable equilibrium is reachable only with deliberate changes. The Club of Rome commissioned the report. It sold in the millions in many languages and started an argument that has not finished. The authors revisited the model in 1992 and again in a 30-year update in 2004.

The report is the best known case of a whole-system model shaping public debate. The history shows how results get read as predictions, how a model's limits get lost in transmission, and how much a small, clearly documented model can carry.
