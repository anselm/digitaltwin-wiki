ERA is a women-led company that develops carbon, biodiversity and women's-empowerment credit projects in Brazil. Under its biodiversity protocol, published on Regen Network's registry, a landholder chooses an umbrella species for the property: a species whose protection also protects many others. The landholder shows that the species is present, using camera traps, footprints, bioacoustics, eDNA or similar methods. Bioacoustics means recording animal sound; eDNA is the DNA animals leave behind in soil or water. Then come interventions meant to improve outcomes for that species and its ecosystem.

Monitoring reports record the evidence, the interventions and local disturbances. An external auditor verifies the reports.

ERA's first project under the protocol is jaguar stewardship in the Pantanal, on 50,000 hectares managed by Instituto Homem Pantaneiro. ERA says it was the first biodiversity credit project in Brazil. Sloth protection in the Mata Atlântica and parakeet protection in the Amazon are listed as further projects, without detail.

A credit's price is set by the cost of conservation: the annual cost of the interventions adopted, which must be reported transparently. The methodology does not apply to aquatic ecosystems.

Version 1 appeared in March 2024. Version 2.2 is dated 1 August 2025. ERA reports more than 75,000 biodiversity credits issued across its projects.
