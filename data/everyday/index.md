Most questions people bring to a place are small. What will grow in this garden? How loud is this street at night? Is the air worse here than two blocks over? Will this lot flood, or bake in summer? Where would solar panels go on this roof? Today the answer usually comes from an expert paid by the hour, from a listing site's score, or from nobody.

Each of these is one address, asked now, by one person, and asked again by millions of others. The data to answer most of them is public: soil surveys, noise and air maps, flood maps, climate records. What is missing is the step from those layers to a plain answer for one place, and a growing number of services sell exactly that step.

The pages here set out each question: who asks it, who they ask today, which data answers it, who already sells an answer, and the formal methods and standards behind it. Each also says what a small look ahead would add, such as the same garden under the climate expected in twenty years, because that is where a model of a place does something a lookup cannot.

The questions share their data. Soil, sun, water, heat, noise and traffic answer several of them at once, and together they are most of what a model of a whole town needs. The [scenarios](/scenarios/) are the large decisions; these are the small ones, and the layers they need are the same.
