Horst Rittel was a design theorist, born in Berlin on 14 July 1930 and died in Heidelberg on 9 July 1990.[^1] He was professor of design methodology at the Ulm School of Design from 1958 to 1963.[^1] From 1963 until his death he was professor of the science of design at the University of California, Berkeley, and from 1973 he was also a professor in the Faculty for Architecture and Town Planning at the University of Stuttgart.[^1] He counted planning, engineering and policy making as forms of design.[^1]

He popularised the term wicked problem in the mid-1960s for the ill-defined problems of planning.[^1] He and [Melvin Webber](/people/melvin-webber/) published "Dilemmas in a General Theory of Planning" in Policy Sciences in 1973.[^2] It argues that the search for scientific bases for social policy is bound to fail, because policy problems are wicked and science has developed to deal with tame ones.[^2] Such problems cannot be definitively described, and there are no solutions "in the sense of definitive and objective answers".[^2] For working on them he devised the issue-based information system (IBIS), conceived in 1968, a method that structures design as argument over issues.[^1]

The 1973 paper is discussed in the article on [Bogucki's critique of incrementalism](/articles/bogucki-incrementalism-complexity/).
