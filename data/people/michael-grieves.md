Michael Grieves works on product lifecycle management (PLM), the handling of a product's information from design to retirement.[^1] After a degree in computer engineering at Michigan State he took an MBA at Oakland University and then a doctorate at Case Western Reserve.[^1] He is executive director of the Digital Twin Institute, and was chief scientist of advanced manufacturing at the Florida Institute of Technology.[^1,3] AIAA's profile says he has consulted or done research for NASA, Boeing, Unilever, Newport News Shipbuilding and General Motors.[^3]

In his 2014 white paper he writes that he introduced the concept of a virtual, digital equivalent of a physical product in 2003, in his University of Michigan executive course on PLM.[^2] The name Digital Twin came later, in his book Virtually Perfect; he attributes it to John Vickers of NASA, a colleague.[^2] In his model the physical product sits in real space and the virtual product in virtual space, tied together by links of data and information.[^2] With Vickers he later wrote a chapter on the digital twin and unpredictable, undesirable emergent behaviour in complex systems.[^1]

The wiki's [history of the digital twin](/articles/history-of-the-digital-twin/) gives his account of where the term came from.
