Gordon Blair is a Distinguished Professor of Distributed Systems in the School of Computing and Communications at Lancaster University, now emeritus.[^1] He took his BSc and PhD in computer science at Strathclyde in 1980 and 1983.[^1,2] His research is on the middleware that underpins complex distributed systems, including reflective and adaptive middleware, model-driven engineering and interoperability, and he is a co-author of the textbook Distributed Systems: Concepts and Design.[^1,2]

He has applied this to environmental science: how cloud computing, the Internet of Things and data science can help it respond to climate change.[^1] He co-founded the Centre of Excellence in Environmental Data Science (CEEDS), a joint initiative of Lancaster and the UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology, and held an EPSRC Senior Fellowship in Digital Technology and Living with Environmental Change.[^1] In 2019 he and Keith Beven, with others, revisited Beven's idea of "models of everywhere".[^3]

This wiki carries his 2021 opinion piece [Digital twins of the natural environment](/articles/blair-digital-twins-of-the-natural-environment/).[^3] The 2026 paper [Digital twins as a tool for ecosystem research](/articles/digital-twins-for-ecosystem-research/) builds on its "jam sandwich" of building blocks.
