Paul N. Edwards is a historian of information infrastructures, especially the systems that produce knowledge about the climate.[^1] He took his PhD in the History of Consciousness at the University of California, Santa Cruz, in 1988.[^1] He taught for 19 years at the University of Michigan, where he is now emeritus Professor of Information and History.[^1,2] Until he retired in January 2026 he directed Stanford's Program on Science, Technology and Society.[^1]

MIT Press published his books The Closed World in 1996 and A Vast Machine: Computer Models, Climate Data, and the Politics of Global Warming in 2010, and Changing the Atmosphere, which he co-edited with Clark Miller, in 2001.[^1] He was one of the lead authors of Working Group I of the IPCC's Sixth Assessment Report, released in August 2021, and co-edits the MIT Press book series Infrastructures.[^1] In 2025 he received the John Desmond Bernal Prize of the Society for Social Studies of Science.[^1]

He wrote [A Vast Machine](/media/a-vast-machine/).
