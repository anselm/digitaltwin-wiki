Donella "Dana" Meadows was born in Elgin, Illinois, on 13 March 1941 and died of meningitis on 20 February 2001.[^1,2] She took a degree in chemistry at Carleton College and a PhD in biophysics at Harvard, then became a research fellow at MIT, on a team in the department created by [Jay Forrester](/people/jay-forrester/).[^1] She taught at Dartmouth College from 1972 for 29 years.[^1] She was a Pew Scholar in Conservation and Environment in 1991 and a MacArthur Fellow in 1994.[^1]

She was on the MIT team that built World3, the global computer model behind [The Limits to Growth](/media/the-limits-to-growth/), and was the book's lead author, with Dennis Meadows, Jørgen Randers and William W. Behrens III.[^1] Jim Schley's eulogy says the book sold nine million copies in twenty-eight languages.[^2] With Dennis Meadows she set up the Balaton Group in 1982, a network of researchers on resources, systems modelling and sustainability that meets each autumn at Lake Balaton in Hungary.[^1] She founded the Sustainability Institute, which now operates as the Academy for Systems Change.[^1]

Her 1999 essay Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System ranks the places where a small shift in a system can produce large changes, from parameters at the weak end to paradigms at the strong end.[^1,3] Her primer [Thinking in Systems](/media/thinking-in-systems/), on stocks, flows, feedback and leverage, was completed from her draft and published in 2008.[^1]

[Incrementalism and complexity](/articles/bogucki-incrementalism-complexity/) draws on her leverage points, and the entry on [tipping points](/terms/tipping-points/) uses the language of feedback her primer teaches.
