David Hillel Gelernter, born on 5 March 1955, is an American computer scientist and writer who joined the Yale computer science faculty in 1982.[^1] In the 1980s he and Nicholas Carriero designed Linda, a system for coordinating parallel programs through a shared tuple space, which Bill Joy cited as the inspiration for parts of JavaSpaces and Jini.[^1] In 1993 he was badly injured by a mail bomb sent by Ted Kaczynski.[^1]

His book Mirror Worlds predicted that small software programs would be joined by large public software works.[^2] Its chief example is the mirror world: a screen showing an image of your city with its traffic moving, or the state of a whole organisation at this second, through which people could explore the real thing in depth.[^2] He helped found Mirror Worlds Technologies, whose Scopeware software, released in 2001, used ideas from the book to organise a user's files; the company closed in 2004.[^1]

The wiki's entry on [Mirror Worlds](/media/mirror-worlds/) covers the book, and [the history of the digital twin](/articles/history-of-the-digital-twin/) starts with it.
