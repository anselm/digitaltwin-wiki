Alvin Toffler was an American writer and futurist, born in New York City on 4 October 1928 and died at his home in Los Angeles on 27 June 2016.[^1,2] He wrote most of his books with his wife, Heidi Toffler.[^1,2] Before the books he was a factory worker, a Washington correspondent and a labour columnist at Fortune, and IBM hired him to research and write a paper on the social and organisational impact of computers.[^1] Future Shock appeared in 1970, The Third Wave in 1980 and Powershift in 1990.[^1] He popularised the term "information overload".[^1,2] In 1996 the Tofflers and Tom Johnson co-founded Toffler Associates, an advisory firm.[^1]

In Future Shock he argued that representative government was the political technology of the industrial era, and that new forms were needed to cope with a crushing overload of decisions.[^3] His answer was anticipatory democracy, which he later defined as "a process for combining citizen participation with future consciousness".[^3] He asked Clement Bezold to edit a book reviewing experiments of that kind.[^3] The Institute for Alternative Futures, founded in 1977 by Toffler, Jim Dator and Bezold to promote foresight and anticipatory democracy, closed at the end of 2019.[^4]

He is the author of [Future Shock](/media/future-shock/) and wrote the introduction to [Anticipatory Democracy](/media/anticipatory-democracy/), edited by [Clement Bezold](/people/clement-bezold/). The history of anticipatory democracy in [foresight and anticipation](/terms/foresight-and-anticipation/) starts with his work.
