Jess Cockerill's story for ScienceAlert reports the [PNAS study](/articles/immense-but-not-immutable-deep-pelagic-decline/) that found the Gulf of Mexico's midwater animals down by around 60% since the Deepwater Horizon spill of 2010. It recalls the spill's scale: 87 days before the well was capped, with about 7.7 billion standard cubic feet of gas released along with the oil, and about 770,000 gallons of the dispersant Corexit 9500A injected near the wellhead. It points to a 2014 study that found oil and dispersant together more toxic to zooplankton than oil alone.

The story adds two threads the study does not settle. Researchers at sea have reported thin whales and sperm whales moving erratically, which would fit a loss of the squid and fish they eat, but nothing in the study shows it. And the animals that rise each night and sink each day carry carbon down with them, so fewer of them might mean less carbon moved to the deep; the story offers that as background, not as a finding.

The story gives the oil released as about 134 million gallons. The study's press release gives 4 to 4.9 million barrels, which at 42 gallons a barrel is 168 to 206 million gallons; the two figures do not agree.
