The World Database on Protected Areas (WDPA) is a joint project of the UN Environment Programme and the International Union for Conservation of Nature. The UN Environment Programme World Conservation Monitoring Centre in Cambridge manages it, and Protected Planet is its public site. Other effective area-based conservation measures (OECMs) are places outside formal protected areas that still conserve nature. The site says the WDPA is the most up to date and complete source of data on protected areas and OECMs. Governments, non-governmental organisations, landowners and communities submit the data.

The site also holds the World Database on OECMs, a database of management-effectiveness assessments (GD-PAME) and the IUCN Green List. Thematic pages cover connectivity and equitable governance. Running global statistics in October 2026 counted 506,250 protected areas and 7,749 OECMs. Together they covered 18.69 per cent of land and inland waters and 10.04 per cent of the sea.

The database was established in 1981. The site traces its mandate to a 1959 UN Economic and Social Council resolution calling for a list of national parks and equivalent reserves. Progress on the [30 by 30 target](/mandates/thirty-by-thirty/) is officially measured against the WDPA.
