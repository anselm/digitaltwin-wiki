Bioregional work organises communities, economies and governance around watersheds, landscapes and ecosystems instead of administrative borders.[^2] The site is also called the Bioregional Knowledge Commons, and covers organizations, practices and ideas in that work.[^2] Andrea Farias started and built the site. She is an autonomous researcher and a member of the worker cooperative Motiu.[^2] It grew out of her report "Bioregional Knowledge Commons - A Meta Perspective", which later led to a Bioregional Knowledge Commons working group in partnership with r3.0.[^2]

The site has three parts:[^3]

- **Organizations** is a world map with a pin for each organization, and a card gallery. The gallery can be searched by name and filtered by category (bioregional organizer, funder, learning network, support provider). It can also be filtered by tag and by whether a full case study exists. Each pin or card opens the organization's own page.[^1,3]
- **Ontology** is the shared vocabulary. Broad themes such as governance, finance and land stewardship each hold narrower tags and list their own activities, organizations and sources.[^3]
- **Sources** is the bibliography of reports, essays and talks the entries draw on.[^3]

Organizations on the map include Tarun Bharat Sangh in Rajasthan, Salmon Nation in British Columbia and Regen Sydney.[^1]

Entries are researched from public material: what organizations publish about themselves, reports, case studies and interviews.[^2] The site is free to browse with no login. There is no download.[^1] Entries are added, filled out and corrected over time, and the project is not meant ever to be complete.[^2] Anyone can write to the maintainers to contribute, suggest content or edit their own organization's entry.[^2]
