Bioregional work organises communities, economies and governance around watersheds, landscapes and ecosystems instead of administrative borders. The site is also called the Bioregional Knowledge Commons, and covers organizations, practices and ideas in that work. Andrea Farias started and built the site. She is an autonomous researcher and a member of the worker cooperative Motiu. It grew out of her report "Bioregional Knowledge Commons - A Meta Perspective", which later led to a Bioregional Knowledge Commons working group in partnership with r3.0.

The site has three parts:

- **Organizations** is a world map with a pin for each organization, and a card gallery. The gallery can be searched by name and filtered by category (bioregional organizer, funder, learning network, support provider). It can also be filtered by tag and by whether a full case study exists. Each pin or card opens the organization's own page.
- **Ontology** is the shared vocabulary. Broad themes such as governance, finance and land stewardship each hold narrower tags and list their own activities, organizations and sources.
- **Sources** is the bibliography of reports, essays and talks the entries draw on.

Organizations on the map include Tarun Bharat Sangh in Rajasthan, Salmon Nation in British Columbia and Regen Sydney.

Entries are researched from public material: what organizations publish about themselves, reports, case studies and interviews. The site is free to browse with no login. There is no download. Entries are added, filled out and corrected over time, and the project is not meant ever to be complete. Anyone can write to the maintainers to contribute, suggest content or edit their own organization's entry.
