GRIIDC, the Gulf of Mexico Research Initiative Information and Data Cooperative, keeps the data from research on the Gulf's coasts and open water.[^1,2] It sits at the Harte Research Institute of Texas A&M University-Corpus Christi, and the old Gulf Research Initiative address now leads to it.[^1,3] Searching and downloading are free. Groups that want to store data there pay a fee.[^1]

Each dataset has its own DOI, which makes it citable down to a single cruise.[^2] The 2026 [study of the Gulf's midwater collapse](/articles/immense-but-not-immutable-deep-pelagic-decline/) lists 27 of them: raw echosounder recordings, the logs of the net's sensors, species counts with weights and measurements for each cruise from 2015 to 2023, and the genetic sequences.[^2] Anyone building a model of the Gulf after [an oil spill](/questions/after-an-oil-spill/) can test it against those records.
