TerraTwin is a Horizon Europe research project that runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2028 with a European Union contribution of about 3.16 million euros, which is its whole budget.[^1] Fourteen organisations take part, coordinated by the IT company Netcompany in Luxembourg; they include the Spanish research council CSIC, the Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn in Naples, the Agricultural University of Athens, Ireland's Marine Institute and the Flemish agriculture and fisheries institute ILVO.[^1] It is funded under the EU Mission on Adaptation to Climate Change.[^2]

The project's stated aim is a "Digital Twin of Ecosystem" for understanding how climate change and marine and terrestrial biodiversity affect each other.[^1] The funder's record says existing data on marine, land, soil and atmospheric biodiversity will be used to feed and train it, and lists what it is meant to offer: analytics, anomaly detection, predictive capabilities, feedback loops and policy recommendations.[^1] Three of the listed components are models. One simulates chosen areas under different climate scenarios. One maps where species can live from environmental conditions, using what is known of the physiology of selected species to estimate how they change under the conditions the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change projects. One is a socio-economic model that joins environmental data to patterns of human activity.[^1] The record also lists data harmonisation, a blockchain component for traceability and a graphical interface.[^1]

Everything above is the proposal as the funder records it. As of November 2026, in the project's first year, the two sources show no results, no public software and no named study sites beyond four countries given as project locations: Belgium, Greece, Ireland and Spain.[^2] The record does not say how predictions will be tested against observation. An earlier European project with a similar aim, [BioDT](/twins/biodt/), finished in 2025 and published its prototypes and an account of what proved hard.
