Virtual Singapore covers the whole city-state and is part of its Smart Nation effort.[^1,2] Three agencies work on it together: the National Research Foundation, the Singapore Land Authority and the Government Technology Agency (GovTech), with the Land Authority supplying the topographic mapping data and GovTech the information technology.[^2] The software is Dassault Systèmes' 3DEXPERIENCity, which runs on Dassault's 3DEXPERIENCE platform.[^1,2]

The model is semantically enriched: each object is labelled by type, such as building, roof, wall or window, and it is meant to connect to real-time data from the national sensor network.[^2] Dassault says agencies share it to see how the city develops over time with population growth and new construction.[^1]

GovTech listed the intended uses in 2017:[^2]

- finding roof and wall surfaces of a given orientation to estimate solar energy yield
- modelling flooding to plan mitigation
- simulating pollution, wind flow and urban heat islands from environmental sensor data
- assessing the traffic and pollution effects of a new highway or stadium before it is built
- finding safe cycling routes
- planning drone flight paths[^2]

A reader cannot install or buy Virtual Singapore: it is a government system built on a commercial product.[^1,2] In March 2017 GovTech said it was targeted for government agencies in 2018, with public access to follow progressively.[^2] At that point the districts of Yuhua and Teck Ghee had been modelled, with Ang Mo Kio next and the rest of the island to follow from mid-2017.[^2] Dassault's customer story, which is undated, says the Singapore government uses the model.[^1]
