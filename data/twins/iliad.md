The European Commission funds Iliad under grant agreement 101037643.[^1] The project combines earth observation, modelling and digital infrastructure to predict future developments at sea.[^1] Its goal is ocean twins that are interoperable and data-intensive, and that can be federated: linked so that separate twins work together.[^1] The project presented this work at the OCEANS 2025 conference in Brest.[^1]

The Iliad Digital Twins of the Ocean Marketplace is a catalogue in the style of an app store.[^2] It lists digital twins, models and components for sectors such as renewable energy, fisheries and citizen science.[^2] A product can be a dataset, script, training material, documentation or a video.[^2] Products are filed by theme, region, type and price.[^2,3] A Twin Lab holds work-in-progress twins for feedback.[^2] The marketplace also lays out a pipeline for building a twin in three parts: data collection, twin creation components and example twins.[^3]

The Iliad Academy, launched in July 2025, offers a course on planning and building a digital twin of the ocean.[^1] The project also runs policy toolkits and competitions for young professionals.[^1]

The marketplace has both free and paid products.[^2] Any visitor can create an account and request access to list products.[^2] An administrator approves listings, and later changes, before they go live.[^2]
