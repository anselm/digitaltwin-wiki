Rancho Cordova is a city of about 85,000 people in the Greater Sacramento region.[^1] In January the city and the Human Machine Collaboration Institute (HMCI) launched AiR Rancho Cordova, a programme to grow a local AI and robotics economy.[^1] The city put $5 million into it over three years.[^1]

The twin is the base layer of that programme.[^1] Over a few days in the summer of 2026 Niantic Spatial flew drones over the city's Innovation Region, about four square kilometres, and reconstructed it in 3D.[^1] Niantic says the model is detailed enough to read a building sign and measure the width of a street.[^1] HMCI already runs traffic simulations on the model.[^1] Capturing the rest of the city is the announced next step.[^1]

Three groups are meant to share the model.[^1] Robotics companies get a real street to test on before they deploy.[^1] Researchers at UC Davis and Sacramento State get a common dataset.[^1] City staff can walk stakeholders through a proposed change, or try a traffic scenario without closing a lane.[^1]

Niantic argues that drone capture and automated reconstruction have made a city twin affordable for a mid-sized place, where it used to take years of survey work.[^1] The announcement gives no price for the capture and does not say whether the model or its data will be open.[^1]
