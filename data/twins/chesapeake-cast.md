CAST is the Chesapeake Bay Program's Watershed Model, reached through a web interface. The program's suite of models divides the 64,000-square-mile watershed into thousands of segments to replicate the Bay's conditions and predict how it responds to pollutant loads, land-use change and changing environmental conditions. CAST is the time-averaged form of the Phase 6 Watershed Model: a user chooses a geographic area such as a county, adds best management practices for developed land, septic systems, natural land, agriculture, animals and manure transport, sets costs or uses defaults, and the tool returns nitrogen, phosphorus and sediment loads at the edge of stream and delivered to the tidal Bay, with costs, on screen and in downloadable tables.

It exists for the Chesapeake Bay Total Maximum Daily Load that the US Environmental Protection Agency issued in 2010 for New York, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, the District of Columbia and Virginia. States and counties use it to write their Watershed Implementation Plans and two-year milestones, to compare scenarios with their allocations, and to find where practices cut the most load for the least money. An Ecosystem Benefits Browser shows the co-benefits of those practices.

What is open: the site offers source data tables (load sources, agencies, practices, animals, geographic references, delivery factors), practice costs, shapefiles, monitoring maps, public reports and the Phase 6 model documentation, calibration plots and the scientific review. Any user may see the data behind the TMDL and each state's latest progress scenario. Creating scenarios requires a login, and the about page says all who request one are granted it. Nothing on the pages read states a licence for the data or says whether the model code is published.

Status: in operation. The first version was built in 2011 by Jessica R. Rigelman of J7 LLC and Olivia H. Devereux of Devereux Consulting with EPA funding; the pages show Phase 6 version 8.5.0 as the model of record, CAST-23 as the planned last Phase 6 update, and a Phase 7 suite of models in development.
