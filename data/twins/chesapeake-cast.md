The Bay Program's suite of models divides the 64,000-square-mile watershed into thousands of segments.[^2] The models replicate the Bay's conditions and predict how it responds to pollutant loads, land-use change and changing environmental conditions.[^2] CAST is the time-averaged form of the Phase 6 Watershed Model.[^3]

A user chooses a geographic area such as a county and adds best management practices, the measures that reduce pollution running off the land.[^2] Practices cover developed land, septic systems, natural land, agriculture, animals and manure transport. Costs can be set or left at defaults.[^2] The tool returns nitrogen, phosphorus and sediment loads at the edge of stream and delivered to the tidal Bay, with costs, on screen and in downloadable tables.[^2]

CAST serves the Chesapeake Bay Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL), which the US Environmental Protection Agency issued in 2010 for six states and the District of Columbia.[^2] A TMDL is a pollution budget: the most of a pollutant a water body can take and still meet water-quality standards.[^4] States and counties use CAST to write their Watershed Implementation Plans and two-year milestones and to compare scenarios with their allocations.[^2] They also use it to find where practices cut the most load for the least money.[^2] An Ecosystem Benefits Browser shows the co-benefits of those practices.[^1]

The site offers source data tables on load sources, agencies, practices, animals, geographic references and delivery factors.[^1] It also has practice costs, shapefiles, monitoring maps and public reports, along with the Phase 6 model documentation, calibration plots and scientific review.[^1,3] Any user may see the data behind the TMDL and each state's latest progress scenario.[^2] Creating scenarios requires a login, granted to all who request one.[^2]

The first version was built in 2011 by Jessica R. Rigelman of J7 LLC and Olivia H. Devereux of Devereux Consulting, with EPA funding.[^2] Phase 6 version 8.5.1 is the model of record.[^1,3] CAST-23 is planned as the last Phase 6 update, and a Phase 7 suite of models is in development.[^3]
