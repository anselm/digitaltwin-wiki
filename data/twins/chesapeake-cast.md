The Bay Program's suite of models divides the 64,000-square-mile watershed into thousands of segments. The models replicate the Bay's conditions and predict how it responds to pollutant loads, land-use change and changing environmental conditions. CAST is the time-averaged form of the Phase 6 Watershed Model.

A user chooses a geographic area such as a county and adds best management practices, the measures that reduce pollution running off the land. Practices cover developed land, septic systems, natural land, agriculture, animals and manure transport. Costs can be set or left at defaults. The tool returns nitrogen, phosphorus and sediment loads at the edge of stream and delivered to the tidal Bay, with costs, on screen and in downloadable tables.

CAST serves the Chesapeake Bay Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL), which the US Environmental Protection Agency issued in 2010 for six states and the District of Columbia. A TMDL is a pollution budget: the most of a pollutant a water body can take and still meet water-quality standards. States and counties use CAST to write their Watershed Implementation Plans and two-year milestones and to compare scenarios with their allocations. They also use it to find where practices cut the most load for the least money. An Ecosystem Benefits Browser shows the co-benefits of those practices.

The site offers source data tables on load sources, agencies, practices, animals, geographic references and delivery factors. It also has practice costs, shapefiles, monitoring maps and public reports, along with the Phase 6 model documentation, calibration plots and scientific review. Any user may see the data behind the TMDL and each state's latest progress scenario. Creating scenarios requires a login, granted to all who request one.

The first version was built in 2011 by Jessica R. Rigelman of J7 LLC and Olivia H. Devereux of Devereux Consulting, with EPA funding. Phase 6 version 8.5.0 is the model of record. CAST-23 is planned as the last Phase 6 update, and a Phase 7 suite of models is in development.
