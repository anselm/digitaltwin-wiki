The European Commission launched the European Digital Twin of the Ocean in 2022 as part of the EU Mission "Restore Our Ocean and Waters".[^2] Its core is EDITO, led by Mercator Ocean International and the Flanders Marine Institute (VLIZ) on behalf of the Commission.[^1,2] The 2022 design has three blocks: a data lake (one store for raw data of many kinds), a live model on high-performance computing, and a front-end simulation layer for user applications.[^3] EDITO is meant to be compatible with Destination Earth, the EU's digital twin of the Earth system.[^2,3]

Two Horizon Europe projects, EDITO-Infra and EDITO-Model Lab, built the first integrated version between 2022 and 2025.[^2] It combined data from the Copernicus Marine Service, EMODnet and other European observing systems.[^2] A first demonstration version was presented at the UN Ocean Conference in Nice in 2025.[^2] A second phase, from 2025 to 2028 under EU grant agreement 101227771, is meant to make it a fully operational system driven by its users.[^1,2]

The decision-support applications are framed as questions: a marine plastic tracker, a turtle drift tracker, a sargassum drift tracker, coastal erosion and nature-based solutions, tuna futures, seasonal chlorophyll forecasts, tide lines from earth observation, and ocean carbon.[^1] Another, GLONET, is an AI ocean forecasting system.[^1] Onboarded projects include:

- SEADITO, social-ecological models with case studies in the Baltic Sea, North Sea and Mediterranean[^4]
- SURIMI, nine socio-ecological fisheries models[^4]
- FOCCUS, coastal forecasting[^4]
- CoCliCo, an open-source web platform on coastal risk from sea-level rise[^4]
- ACCIBERG's OpenBerg, an iceberg-drift tool[^4]

EDITO is free and public.[^1] Copernicus Marine and EMODnet data can be explored in the viewer without signing up.[^1] A registered user can process data on demand, run predefined services and work in groups.[^1] Registered users can also publish data, services, processes and tutorials to the catalogues, and connect to EuroHPC, GitHub, GitLab or Kaggle.[^1] Computing resources are requested through a form and granted after review.[^1] The project also runs calls for financial support to third parties and onboards outside research projects.[^1]
