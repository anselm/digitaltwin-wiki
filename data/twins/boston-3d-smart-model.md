The City of Boston Planning Department took over the staff and responsibilities of the Boston Planning & Development Agency.[^1] Its GIS Lab maintains the model, which has three components: terrain, groundplan and 3D building models.[^3] It began with the city's 2011 planimetric data (flat map outlines of buildings and other features) and is updated regularly from urban design models, CyberCity 3D and lidar.[^2] The lab manages it through the citySchema model stewardship process, a 3D model architecture and digital asset management system developed by Paul Cote (cityschema.org).[^2] The city calls citySchema open source.[^2]

The city uses it to let planners, policymakers and the public see proposed development in 3D and assess its effects.[^2] A citywide view is online, and there is also a physical model room.[^2,4]

The whole model can be downloaded, divided into tiles.[^3] Each component comes in open data formats, and each tile also has an all-in-one SketchUp model of all three.[^3] A citywide SketchUp tile grid places the tiles.[^3] The data is released under the Open Data Commons Public Domain Dedication and License (PDDL).[^3]
