COBALT, the Collaborative for Bioregional Action, Learning, and Transformation, is a non-profit in Portland, Maine, led by Glenn Page, that works on the Gulf of Maine and in particular on eelgrass conservation and restoration in Casco Bay.[^1] The Buckminster Fuller Institute's Design Lab supports it, and the institute's page says COBALT is "developing what could be the world's first Bioregional Digital Twin".[^1] The project is run by COBALT with Zedaxis Group, a UK company that sells 3D geovisualisation, digital twins and data-driven games.[^1,3] It starts with two places, the Gulf of Maine and the River Tay watershed in Scotland.[^1]

The page describes the twin as "a vast, rich, living 3D model of the bioregion that people can explore and interact with, and which drives real world action through play mechanics".[^1] It lists three parts: a collection of scientific, cultural, economic and geophysical data about the place, which it calls sovereign and decentralised; the turning of that data into knowledge through crowdsourcing and partnerships with academic, technology, AI and Earth observation organisations; and a single 3D interface for exploring the result.[^1] The stated purpose is to engage the widest range of people and "inspire them to perform direct stewardship action in the real world".[^1]

What exists in public is a story map that the page calls "a rough first draft" of a proto-bioregional digital twin. It brings together data sets on the Casco Bay bioregion's governance, food systems, eelgrass beds and Wabanaki place-names, and on selected local initiatives.[^1] An announcement for a six-day kayak and bicycle expedition for organisations working on coastal resilience says the organisers would "introduce a bioregional digital twin that will serve as the holding space for the data that is collected" about who is doing what and where.[^2]

None of the three sources names a simulation, a forecast or a model that is run forward in time, and none says that the twin is updated from measurements. By its own description this is an atlas and a means of participation, not a predictive model. The sources give no date for a working version and no account of who has used the story map or to what effect. The wiki's pages on [putting a model in front of people](/communication/) cover the part of the work it does describe.
