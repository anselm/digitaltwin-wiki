# Who this is for

The root rule. Every other choice, what gets an entry, what gets promoted,
what sits in the nav, how an entry is worded, follows from it. The data
model is in `data-design.md`, the words in `curation.md`; this is the test
both serve.

## The patrons

The site is for three kinds of guest, in this order:

1. **The person with a mandate.** Someone at a city, a utility, an agency,
   a watershed council or a non-profit who has been told to "get us a
   digital twin" or "model our flood risk", and who is not a modeller.
   They need to know what question they are really asking, what kind of
   model answers it, who has done it for a place like theirs, roughly what
   it costs and takes, what to ask a vendor, and where the data is. They
   are the reason the site exists.
2. **The builder.** A student, a researcher or an engineer who can code
   and wants to make one. They need open tools with real documentation,
   the data sources, worked examples to copy, and the papers and books
   that set a practice.
3. **The practitioner keeping up.** Someone already in the field who
   wants to know what is new and credible, and who may use the site as a
   citation or feed its exports to their own tools.

Not patrons: vendors looking for placement; readers who want every mention
of the subject; the editors. The scanner, the inbox and the Sites section
are for the editors and stay out of the guest's way.

## The test

An entry is good enough when one of the three would open it and be better
off. By kind:

- An **engine** says what it is for, who uses it, how it runs and what it
  costs, from the maker's own page. A name with a one-line description
  is not an entry.
- A **twin** names a place, says whether it runs, and who runs it.
- A **data source** says what it covers, how often it updates, how to get
  it and on what terms.
- A **guide** or **question** is written for the first patron: no term
  used before it is explained, and it ends with what to do next.
- A **paper** or **book** sets a practice a reader could follow, or
  explains a thing the wiki relies on. A result on its own is a news item,
  not an entry.
- A **story** or **event** is included because a patron would learn from
  it, not because it happened.

If the sources read do not support that bar, the entry stays a draft.
Drafts are the editors' material and are not shown to guests.

## What follows

- **Fewer, better.** A section with twelve good entries serves the first
  patron better than one with eighty names. Coverage is the scanner's
  job; the site's job is to illuminate.
- **Plain words first.** Titles, descriptions and the first paragraph are
  written for the person with a mandate; the rigour sits beneath. "Digital
  twin" is shorthand and is not leaned on.
- **Start by need, not by kind.** The home page should let a guest say
  "I have a mandate", "I want to build one", or "what is new", and land
  somewhere useful in one click.
- **Everything cites.** The third patron must be able to trust and quote
  an entry; the sources list is not optional.
